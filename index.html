Hadi Reisizadeh

Postdoctoral Associate, Optimization for AI Lab, University of Minnesota

Hi, welcome to my page! I am a Postdoctoral Associate at the Optimization for Artificial Intelligence (OptimAI) Lab at the University of Minnesota, working with Prof. Mingyi Hong. I received my Ph.D. and M.Sc. in Electrical and Computer Engineering (with a minor in Computer Science) from the University of Minnesota, Twin Cities, and my B.Sc. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Isfahan University of Technology. During my PhD, I was a Research Intern on the Data Trust Research Team at Seagate Technology.

My research develops optimization and information-theoretic principles for safe and efficient machine learning, especially for large language models. I study how models learn and forget, and build methods grounded in theory that hold up in practice. My current research interests include:

  • 🔍
    Trustworthy Evaluation: I showed that sensitive knowledge can resurface under probabilistic decoding and introduced leak@k, a meta-metric to quantify worst-case knowledge leakage across k generations. I also showed that unlearned models still encode sensitive knowledge in their hidden states, allowing it to be recovered through different attacks.
  • 🧹
    LLM Unlearning: I proposed BLUR, a bi-level optimization framework for LLM unlearning prioritizing the forget objective over the retain loss; RULE, an unlearning method designed to minimize leakage under leak@k; and PARS, removing knowledge at the hidden-state level and is more robust to various attacks.
  • 🌐
    Distributed Optimization: I characterized convergence guarantees for agents that learn together over time-varying networks with imperfect, lossy communication.
  • 📐
    Theoretical Foundations: I use optimization and information theory to expose the fundamental limits of existing algorithms and design rigorous alternatives.

Selected Publications

  1. Preprint
    Do LLMs Really Forget? Hidden-State Leakage in Model Unlearning and How to Fix it
    Hadi Reisizadeh, Jiajun Ruan, Sijia Liu, Mingyi Hong
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.36612, 2026
    arXiv PDF Code
    LLM unlearning is usually evaluated only on the model's outputs, which can create an illusion of forgetting. We show that sensitive information persists in the model's hidden representations even after output-level leakage has been eliminated. Using generative probe decoders that measure layer-wise information leakage, together with theoretical analysis, we find substantial recoverable information in hidden states across the TOFU, MUSE and WMDP benchmarks, despite successful output-level unlearning metrics. To close this gap, we propose PARS (Probe-Adversarial Representation Suppression), an unlearning objective that adversarially minimizes the information that can be extracted from hidden representations across transformer layers. PARS outperforms baseline methods at controlling both output-level and representation-level leakage, and gives stronger erasure against adversarial probing and relearning attacks.

  2. ICML'26
    Leak@k: Unlearning Does Not Make LLMs Forget Under Probabilistic Decoding
    Hadi Reisizadeh, Jiajun Ruan, Yiwei Chen, Soumyadeep Pal, Sijia Liu, Mingyi Hong
    International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026
    arXiv PDF Code
    Standard unlearning evaluations use deterministic (greedy) decoding and suggest that sensitive knowledge has been removed. We show that this knowledge reliably resurfaces once the model is sampled with standard probabilistic decoding. We introduce leak@k, a metric for the probability that forgotten knowledge reappears within k samples, and use it to evaluate existing methods on TOFU, MUSE and WMDP, finding persistent leakage. We then propose RULE (Robust Unlearning under LEak@k), which achieves zero leakage across many samples on TOFU and outperforms state-of-the-art methods on MUSE for most sampling budgets.

  3. EACL'26
    BLUR: A Bi-Level Optimization Approach for LLM Unlearning
    Hadi Reisizadeh, Jinghan Jia, Zhiqi Bu, Bhanukiran Vinzamuri, Anil Ramakrishna, Kai-Wei Chang, Volkan Cevher, Sijia Liu, Mingyi Hong
    Conference of the European Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics (EACL), 2026
    arXiv Paper Code
    Unlearning knowledge and capabilities acquired during training is increasingly important for regulatory compliance and responsible AI. Existing methods typically combine a forget loss and a retain loss into one objective, which degrades performance because of the trade-off between the two. We argue that unlearning has a natural hierarchy in which forgetting takes priority, and formulate it as a bi-level problem: the lower level minimizes the forget loss, while the upper level preserves the model's utility. The resulting algorithm, BLUR, consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across unlearning tasks, models and metrics.

  4. IEEE TAC
    Distributed Optimization Over Time-Varying Graphs With Imperfect Sharing of Information
    Hadi Reisizadeh, Behrouz Touri, Soheil Mohajer
    IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, 2022
    arXiv PDF
    We study strongly convex distributed optimization, where a set of agents collaboratively solve a separable problem while sharing information over time-varying networks with lossy links. We propose a two-time-scale decentralized gradient descent algorithm, in which one time scale handles the degradation of incoming information and the other handles local gradient updates. With appropriately chosen step sizes, the agents' estimates converge to the global optimum at rate O(T-1/2). Along the way we develop new tools for handling diminishing average weights over time-varying graphs.
